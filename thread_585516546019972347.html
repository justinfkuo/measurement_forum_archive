Pete, I do believe we should explore the possibility of using the GPS as a ...

justinkuo #1 • Feb 22, 2008 at 12:04 PM
Pete,

I do believe we should explore the possibility of using the GPS as a tool to measure trail and cross-country courses and to help measure road courses. Two years ago I purchased the Garmin GPSMAP 60CX unit. My previous GPS was the Garmin eTrex. These days, I find it difficult to live without my GPS and move it back and forth between my car, bicycle and backpak. I use it to track the course I'm measuring, to get to the course by car and to follow the correct course while measuring.

On a clear night in August 2006, I tested the Garmin GPSMAP 60CX unit at work. My desk faces south, and is directly beside a ceiling high plate glass window with an nearly unobstructed view (ie., trees were 25 meters away) of the outdoors. There is a 3 foot high stone railing that is 5 feet south of the glass. There is also a 5 foot overhang at the roof, 25 feet above the ground.





I positioned my GPS so the antenna was leaning directly against the window and allowed it to remain in that position for 4 hours and 20 minutes (from 2302 to 0322). Without moving the unit, the GPS recorded movement of 2.8 kilometers or 0.7 km per hour.



On another clear night, I placed the same GPS unit on the roof of the building and left it there for eight hours. There was nothing between the GPS and the sky and the GPS recorded absolutely no movement.

I know a lot of the drifting problem is due to the software in the GPS and updates may have corrected that issue. (I really need to test my GPS again to see if the recent updates make a difference. Maybe someone could experiment with their GPS unit.)

That drifting problem is something to keep in mind when using the GPS. It may be less of a problem if the GPS was raised higher, had a better view of the sky, and was used when the sky was clear and cloud-free. I know some folks use the GPS above their head. An external antenna may be attached to the Garmin GPSMAP 60CX. That antenna could be attached to a pole allowing the user to see the display.

Thank you. -- Justin
