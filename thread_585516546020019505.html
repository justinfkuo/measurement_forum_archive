It's not too hard to measure when there is so much traffic that it is barel...

bobthurston #1 • Unknown date
It's not too hard to measure when there is so much traffic that it is barely moving. I did learn that with so many lanes of cars in the road, I would not be able to "sight" across to my favorite landmarks for switching from one side to the other-- I like to pick a nice perpendicular line near the edge of the street, like a storm drain edge or something-- but on this day I had to use the light poles, the only thing you could see above the traffic.

If anything you may tend to measure distances as being slightly shorter than the SPR when you measure by only using road edges (always on the shorter side when road is curving). For instance there was a place where cars were right next to the curb, and I had to measure on top of the curb. If you are laying out a course this is not so much of a problem but if you are measuring a distance being subtracted due to a course adjustment or emergency, this can be a problem-- if you understate the distance being subtracted you might end up overstating the final course length.
