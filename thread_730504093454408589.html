How to use data from pre- and post- measurement calibrations

alanjones #1 • 5/5/25 @ 2:34 PM

I decided to see what response Google Gemini would give to the query, "What is the Jones Counter." I won't reproduce the response here unless someone requests it. I felt the response was very good and quite complete except for one item in the response which I quote here:

  • Recalibration: After measuring the course, the bicycle is ridden over the calibration course again to check for any changes in the wheel diameter (due to temperature, tire pressure, etc.) that might affect the accuracy of the measurement. An average of the calibration values before and after the measurement is used to determine the final course length.  

Isn't it true that the correct procedure is to use the calibration value which results in the longest course. Or has this changed?

Alan Jones

Admin #2 • 5/5/25 @ 2:46 PM

Using the average of the constants is also permitted.

  1. The constant for the day is either the working constant or the finish constant, whichever is larger. Although measurements using the average of the working and finish constants will be accepted, it is strongly recommended to use the larger constant.

Race Resources LLC #4 • 5/6/25 @ 2:10 PM

Either the "longest course constant" or the average constant are allowed.

An example of when the "longest" makes no sense: I once measured an 8K on a hot summer afternoon. I barely finished before a large thunderstorm blew through. When the storm finally passed, I drove to the nearby call course for post-cal. The air temperature had dropped just shy of 30℉ as I rode the cal course. The post-cal numbers were much higher than the pre-cal. Bothe the pre-cal and the measurement were made on hot pavement. Had I used this "cold" count, I would have had to return to the course and lengthen the Finish. I used the average count instead, which kept me from having to make an adjustment.

My two rides were within a small number of counts - I don't remember the number - but the course was nearly empty of traffic when I measured. I had a lot of confidence in the distance. I know there are other examples of when the average seems best. One is when measuring longer courses, when the temperature changes more than a trivial amount during the rides.
